# The pixel set

Thirty-seven icons keyed out of four reference sheets Ashley supplied,
cut by colour distance from each sheet's own corners, split into
connected regions, and labelled. Six more were keyed badly and deleted.

They are split into two folders on one criterion: whether putting the
icon on a commercial post exposes Ashley to somebody else's trademark.

## `pixel/` — 20 icons, clear to use

Generic pixel-art objects. A folder, a cursor, an hourglass and a heart
are common visual idioms, not anybody's property.

arcade, biscuit, card, coin, crt, cup, cursor, cursor-line, folder-open,
heart, joystick, lives, loading, menu, pad, plant, sparkle-gold, start,
sunflower

## Cut and thrown away — 6

`eyeball` you asked to go. The other five failed the same test: an icon
you have to explain is not an icon.

Measurably clipped, solid ink running off the border: `eyeball` (8px
left, 6px right), `sparkle-blue` (11px left, 16px right).

Cut by the key rather than the crop box, so the border test could not
see it: `bed` lost its foot and reads as a striped slab, `ghost` has its
tail sliced flat. `heart-small` came out with a soft halo all round and
a squared-off lobe, and `heart` and `lives` already say the same thing.
`heart-real` is a photographic gradient in a pixel set and goes to red
mush at icon size.

## `pixel-flagged/` — 17 icons, your call

These are keyed and labelled and sitting there. They are not wired into
the generator, because each one is somebody's mark and web3ashley posts
are commercial use.

**Characters and brands.** `mushroom` and `mushroom-2` are Nintendo's
Super Mario power-up. `cat` is Pusheen, a licensed character. `handheld`
is a Tamagotchi (Bandai). `sandwich-biscuit` is an Oreo (Mondelez).
Using a licensed character to advertise a business is the clearest kind
of exposure there is.

**Trade dress.** `folder`, `bin`, `hourglass`, `hand`, `cursor-thin`,
`disc`, `desktop`, `desktop-2` and `globe-find` are Microsoft's Windows
95 icons, pixel for pixel, not generic drawings of the same objects.
`dino` and `dino-solid` are Google Chrome's offline dinosaur.
`pad-retro` is a Nintendo controller.

The risk is small and it is real. A retro post using a Windows 95 folder
is common and rarely challenged; the same folder on an ad for web design
services is a different thing, because it implies a connection to
Microsoft. It is your business and your call, which is why they are
extracted rather than deleted.

If you want any of them live, move the file into `pixel/` and add a line
to `assets/js/icons.js`. If you want a clean equivalent instead, say so:
a folder, a bin and an hourglass are half an hour to draw from scratch
and then they are yours.

## Where they came from

Sheet 1 credits **tocaiastudio** and is tagged `#elementoscanva`, so its
contents are Canva elements somebody compiled. Sheet 2 credits **Mergyle
Studio**. Sheets 3 and 4 carry no credit. None of the four is licensed
to Ashley, so the extraction is of the objects rather than of the
sheets, and nothing here reproduces a sheet's layout, arrangement or
credit line.
