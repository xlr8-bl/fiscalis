# Ashley's own photographs

Supplied by Ashley, September 2026. Not stock: these are his to use,
they carry no attribution obligation and no licence to keep track of,
and they are the reason a sheet can stop looking like everybody else's
sheet. The generator reaches for these before the stock library.

Longest side capped at 1600px. The sheets draw at 1080x1350, so nothing
here is upscaled and nothing is a 4000px original sitting in R2.

## Where the type can go

Measured rather than eyeballed: a 9px local standard deviation over each
photograph, thirds counted as `quiet` where it falls under 6. A quiet
third takes type; a busy one does not, whatever it looks like at a
glance. Luminance says which way the type has to run.

| file | quiet | put the type | ink |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sky-arms.jpg` | the whole top third, and the right column all the way down | top, or hard right | light, over sky at lum 86-105 |
| `blue-flat.jpg` | the entire left column | left half | light, over flat blue at lum 86 |
| `black-wall.jpg` | the whole left half | left half | light, over near-black at lum 15 |
| `phone-chair.jpg` | top left and top right corners, and the bottom left | top corners | dark, over white at lum 228 |

`phone-chair` is the only one with a dark-on-light half, and the only
one whose quiet regions are corners rather than a column. It is a
portrait on a white sweep, so it wants type in the corners around the
figure rather than a block beside it.

## A person is in all four

Every one of these carries a recognisable face. Two rules follow, and
they are rules rather than preferences:

They are only for Ashley's own posts. A face on a sheet reads as an
endorsement of whatever the sheet says, and these are not stock models
who signed anything — using one under a claim about somebody else's
business would put a real person's likeness behind a sentence they never
agreed to.

Nothing invented may be set over them. No fabricated quote, no invented
statistic beside the face, and no wording that implies the person
pictured said the thing. This is the same rule the corpus already
applies to the four hook references that carry real faces, and it is
stricter here because these are people Ashley knows.
